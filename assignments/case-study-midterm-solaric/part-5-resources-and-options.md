# Part 5: Additional resources, ramp-up options and recommendation

## 5.1 Additional resources needed to close the forecasting gap

The resources are scaled from the 100 MW reference plant: **resources needed = gap (MW) ÷ 100 × resources of the 100 MW plant**.

The gap is cumulative (Part 3). Each year's figure is the **total extra capacity** Solaric must have in place by that year.

| Year | Gap (MW) | Machinery incl. 15% VAT ($M) | Land (acres) | Manpower (persons) | Water (m³/day) | New capacity added that year (MW) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | 47.65 | 52.60 | 190.6 | 286 | 38.1 | 47.65 |
| 2028 | 50.79 | 56.07 | 203.2 | 305 | 40.6 | 3.14 |
| 2029 | 53.93 | 59.54 | 215.7 | 324 | 43.1 | 3.14 |
| 2030 | **57.07** | **63.01** | **228.3** | **342** | **45.7** | 3.14 |

By 2030 Solaric needs an extra **$63.0M of equipment, about 228 acres of land, about 342 staff and about 46 m³/day of water**. Most of it (47.65 MW, or 83%) is needed **by 2027**.

## 5.2 Unit costs (from Part 4)

| Cost | Per MW | Working |
|---|---|---|
| Capital cost, k (machinery incl. VAT + land) | **$1.2018M per MW** | ($110.40M + $9.78M) ÷ 100 |
| Operating cost, o (manpower + water) | **$17,707 per MW per year** | ($1.76M + $0.011M) ÷ 100 |

Comparison period: **2027–2030**. Capacity-years needed = 47.65 + 50.79 + 53.93 + 57.07 = **209.44 MW-years**.

## 5.3 Three alternative pathways

### Option A: one 100 MW plant in 2027 (leading strategy, build in-house)
Build a complete 100 MW plant before 2027, ahead of demand.

- Capital = 100 × $1.2018M = **$120.18M**
- Operating = 100 MW × 4 years × $17,707 = **$7.08M**
- **Total = $127.26M**
- **Utilisation** of the new capacity: only **48% in 2027, rising to 57% in 2030**. About 43 MW sits idle.
- **For it:** one project and the lowest cost per MW to build. Spare capacity is a **barrier to entry** (lecture 4) and could cover the higher demand scenarios.
- **Against it:** the highest cash outlay, a lot of unused capacity, and the most risk if demand falls short. It also needs 400 acres at once, which is hard given the land shortage in Bangladesh.

### Option B: phased in-house build (leading step in 2027, then tracking)
Build **47.65 MW before 2027** to cover the step-up, then add **about 3.14 MW a year** to keep pace with the target.

- Smaller plants lose some economies of scale, so assume a **10% cost premium** per MW
- Capital = 57.07 × $1.2018M × 1.10 = **$75.44M**
- Operating = 209.44 MW-years × $17,707 = **$3.71M**
- **Total = $79.15M**
- **Utilisation:** about **100%**, because capacity is added as it's needed.
- **For it:** the lowest total cost, no idle capacity, and Solaric keeps the capability in-house. Later phases can be **scaled up or down** if demand turns out higher or lower (Part 2 sensitivity range: 46–71 MW).
- **Against it:** several construction projects to manage, and a 10% higher cost per MW.

### Option C: outsource to EPC or partner firms (buy)
Contract turnkey partners (EPC contractors or joint-venture partners) to supply and run the extra capacity. The partners provide land, equipment and staff.

- Assume a **20% partner margin** on Solaric's in-house costs
- Capital = 57.07 × $1.2018M × 1.20 = **$82.30M**
- Operating = 209.44 × $17,707 × 1.20 = **$4.45M**
- **Total = $86.75M**
- **Utilisation:** about **100%**.
- **For it:** the **fastest** route to the 2027 step-up, because Solaric doesn't have to acquire land itself (the Mymensingh park took almost 2 years to secure 174 acres). Less risk and no new long-term assets.
- **Against it:** a higher cost per MW, less control over quality and schedule, dependence on partners, and no capability built inside Solaric.

## 5.4 Financial comparison

| | Option A: 100 MW plant | **Option B: phased in-house** | Option C: outsource |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital ($M) | 120.18 | **75.44** | 82.30 |
| Operating 2027–2030 ($M) | 7.08 | **3.71** | 4.45 |
| **Total cost ($M)** | 127.26 | **79.15** | 86.75 |
| Cost per MW of 2030 gap ($M/MW) | 2.23 | **1.39** | 1.52 |
| Utilisation of new capacity | 48–57% | **≈100%** | ≈100% |
| Speed to 2027 | Slow (400 acres at once) | Medium | **Fast** |
| Capability kept in-house | Yes | **Yes** | No |

**Option B is the most attractive financially.** It costs **$48.1M less than A** and **$7.6M less than C**.

**Break-even check:** Option C would only beat B if the partner margin were below **9.5%**. Typical EPC margins are higher, so B stays cheaper unless a partner offers an unusually low price.

## 5.5 Recommendation

**Recommend Option B: a phased in-house build.** Build about 48 MW ahead of 2027 (a leading strategy for the big step), then add about 3 MW a year (a tracking strategy) to 2030.

**Why:**
1. **Lowest cost.** About $79M over 2027–2030, the cheapest per MW of the gap.
2. **High utilisation.** Capacity is added as needed. In lecture 4 terms, there's no idle capacity dragging down utilisation, unlike Option A where around 43% of the plant would sit unused.
3. **Strategic value.** Solar installation is Solaric's core business, so it has **high strategic value**. The make-or-buy matrix says **make**, and Solaric builds lasting capability.
4. **Flexibility.** Demand forecasts for 2030 vary (gap of 46–71 MW, Part 2). Phasing lets Solaric adjust the later phases instead of committing everything at once.
5. **Productivity.** Part 4 showed the gap cannot be closed by productivity alone. Option B adds the needed resources while keeping MFP at the plant level of 0.82 MW per $M.

**Risks and how to manage them:**

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Land shortage could delay 2027 capacity | Start land acquisition in 2026. Use barren riverside or char land and large commercial rooftops. Use **Option C as a stop-gap** for any 2027 shortfall |
| 15% VAT raises equipment cost by about 13% per MW | Seek VAT exemption or incentives for renewable projects. Negotiate bulk equipment contracts across all phases |
| Several construction projects to manage | Standardise plant design (lecture 4: uniform output means more effective capacity). Use one equipment supplier framework |
| Demand turns out higher than forecast | Bring later phases forward. Option A becomes more attractive if the higher (PSMP) scenario occurs |

> **Assumptions in this part:** the 10% scale premium (B) and 20% partner margin (C) are estimates. Costs are not discounted. The comparison covers 2027–2030 only. Revenue from spare capacity in Option A is not included.

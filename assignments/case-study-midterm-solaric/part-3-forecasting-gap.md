# Part 3: Forecasting gap, 2027–2030

**Forecasting gap = F series 2 (policy target) − F series 1 (least-squares trend)**

The gap is the extra installed capacity Solaric needs, on top of its business-as-usual growth, to keep its market share if Bangladesh meets the 20% green-power target.

| Year | F series 1 (MWp) | F series 2 (MWp) | **Gap (MWp)** | Gap as % of series 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | 35.54 | 83.19 | **47.65** | 134.06% |
| 2028 | 37.89 | 88.67 | **50.79** | 134.06% |
| 2029 | 40.23 | 94.16 | **53.93** | 134.06% |
| 2030 | 42.57 | 99.64 | **57.07** | 134.06% |

The gaps are calculated from unrounded values, so a few may differ by 0.01 from subtracting the rounded columns. The % column is constant because series 2 for 2027–2029 was built by applying the 2030 % difference (Part 2, step 5).

## What the gap means

1. **Solaric would have to more than double its trend capacity.** By 2030 it needs about **57 MWp** more than its trend line gives, meaning 99.64 MWp instead of 42.57 MWp.

2. **The growth rate needed is nearly four times the historical rate.**
   - Trend (series 1): 30 → 42.57 MWp from 2025 to 2030, about **7.3% a year** (compound).
   - Policy target (series 2): 30 → 99.64 MWp, about **27.1% a year** (compound).

3. **The biggest problem is the 2027 step-up, not the yearly growth.**
   - After 2027, series 2 grows by about 5.5 MWp a year, compared with 2.3 MWp a year on the trend.
   - To reach 83.19 MWp in 2027, Solaric must add about **50 MWp in a single year** over its 2026 trend level of 33.20 MWp. That's more than its entire installed base in 2025.
   - This is why capacity has to be planned **ahead** of demand (a leading capacity strategy, lecture 4): equipment, land, staff and supply contracts must be in place before 2027.

4. **The gap is roughly the size of half a 100 MW plant.** The 2030 gap of about 57 MWp is a little over half the 100 MW reference plant whose input costs are given in Part 4. That is the scale used to work out the extra resources in Part 5.

> **Sensitivity (from Part 2):** with the lower IEEFA demand forecast, the 2030 gap is about **46 MWp**. With the higher PSMP 2016 forecast it's about **71 MWp**. In every scenario the gap is far larger than Solaric's trend growth can cover.

## A. Cheat sheet

### What capacity is
- **Capacity** is the **upper limit (ceiling)** on the load an operating unit can handle: a plant, department, machine or worker.
  - It can be measured in **units** (bicycles assembled per hour) or in **services** (computers upgraded per hour).
- **Bigger capacity means higher capital cost but lower operating cost.** That's why projects often start with a small **pilot** unit, even though it costs more to run.
- **Three key questions:** *what kind* of capacity, *how much*, and *when*.

### Why capacity decisions are strategic
1. **Operating costs.** Matching capacity to demand keeps them low.
2. **Long-term commitment of resources.** Hard or expensive to change once built.
3. **Competitiveness.** Excess capacity, or the ability to add it quickly, is a **barrier to entry** and supports delivery speed.
4. **Ease of management.** Capacity that matches demand is easier to manage.

Globalisation makes these decisions harder: distant supply chains and markets add uncertainty, and long projects need **scenario planning**.

### Measuring capacity

| Measure | Meaning | What reduces it |
|---|---|---|
| **Design capacity** | Maximum output under **ideal** conditions | — |
| **Effective capacity** | Design minus **planned** allowances | Breaks, personal time, product-mix changes, preventive maintenance, scheduling and balancing, scrap |
| **Actual output** | Effective minus **unplanned** losses | Breakdowns, absenteeism, material shortages, quality problems, things outside the manager's control |

Design ≥ Effective ≥ Actual.

- **Efficiency = Actual ÷ Effective × 100%**
- **Utilisation = Actual ÷ Design × 100%**

Effective capacity caps actual output. To raise utilisation, raise effective capacity: fix quality problems, maintain equipment, train staff, improve bottlenecks and cut waste (lean).

### The 8 determinants of effective capacity

| Factor | Example from the slides |
|---|---|
| **Facilities** (size, location, layout, environment) | Boeing 737 MAX: no storage space for finished jets |
| **Product/service** (uniform output means more capacity; a limited menu is faster) | Tesla Model 3: complex design slowed assembly |
| **Process** (quality, rework, changeovers) | Toyota Aichi: retooling for hybrid engines |
| **Human** (training, motivation, absenteeism, turnover) | Foxconn during COVID: labour shortages |
| **Policy** (overtime, 2nd or 3rd shifts allowed or not) | India textile export quotas |
| **Operational** (scheduling, inventory, late deliveries) | Ford JIT software error |
| **Supply chain** (can suppliers and distributors cope?) | Toyota microchip shortage |
| **External** (standards, pollution rules, paperwork, disasters) | Bangladesh garment floods, 2017 |

### Capacity needed
- **Machines needed = Total processing time needed ÷ Time available per machine**
- Time per machine = hours per shift × shifts × working days.
- **Always round up.** 2.9 machines means you buy 3.

### Strategy

| Strategy | When capacity is added |
|---|---|
| **Leading** | **Before** demand grows. Best when adding capacity takes a long time |
| **Following** | **After** demand exceeds capacity |
| **Tracking** | Like following, but in **small steps** that keep pace with demand |

**What strategy is based on:** demand growth and variability, the cost of building and running facilities, technology change, competitors' behaviour, and the availability of capital.

**The 8 steps of capacity planning:**
1. Estimate future requirements.
2. Evaluate existing capacity and find the gaps.
3. Identify alternatives.
4. Do a financial analysis of each.
5. Assess the qualitative issues.
6. Select the best long-term alternative.
7. Implement it.
8. Monitor the results.

**Make-or-buy matrix:**

| | Low cost | High cost |
|---|---|---|
| **High strategic value** | Weigh cost/benefit | **Make** |
| **Low strategic value** | **Buy** | Defer / judgement call |

### Cost-volume analysis
Techniques for evaluating alternatives: cost-volume analysis, financial analysis, decision theory and waiting-line analysis.

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Total cost | TC = FC + v·Q |
| Total revenue | TR = R·Q |
| Profit | **P = Q(R − v) − FC** |
| Contribution margin | **R − v** |
| Volume for a target profit | **Q = (P + FC) ÷ (R − v)** |
| **Break-even point** | **Q_BEP = FC ÷ (R − v)** |
| Make-or-buy indifference | **Q₀ = FC ÷ (Buy price − v)**. Above Q₀ make, below Q₀ buy |

- Below the BEP you make a loss. Above it you make a profit.
- If the **buy price is lower than your variable cost**, just **buy**. Fixed cost doesn't even matter then.

## B. Practice MCQs

**1.** A workshop has a design capacity of 120 units/day, an effective capacity of 100 and an actual output of 85. What are its efficiency and utilisation?
(a) 85% and 70.8%  (b) 70.8% and 85%  (c) 85% and 85%  (d) 117.6% and 141.2%

**2.** Design capacity is 200 units/day. Effective capacity is 90% of design, and efficiency is 90%. What is the utilisation?
(a) 90%  (b) 81%  (c) 180%  (d) 72%

**3.** Which of these lowers **effective** capacity rather than **actual** output?
(a) Machine breakdowns  (b) Absenteeism  (c) Scheduled lunch breaks  (d) A material shortage

**4.** Which of these lowers **actual** output below effective capacity?
(a) Preventive maintenance  (b) Changing the product mix  (c) Coffee breaks  (d) Quality problems

**5.** A plant runs 2 shifts of 8 hours for 250 days a year. Annual demand is 600 units of A at 3 h each, 400 units of B at 4.5 h each, and 1,000 units of C at 1.2 h each. How many machines are needed?
(a) 1  (b) 1.2  (c) 2  (d) 3

**6.** Total processing time needed is 5,800 h a year. A machine works one 8-hour shift for 250 days. How many machines are needed?
(a) 2  (b) 2.9  (c) 3  (d) 5

**7.** Fixed cost is $24,000, price is $15 per unit and variable cost is $9 per unit. What is the break-even point?
(a) 1,600 units  (b) 2,667 units  (c) 4,000 units  (d) 6,000 units

**8.** Using Q7's data, what is the profit at 5,000 units?
(a) $6,000  (b) $30,000  (c) $51,000  (d) −$6,000

**9.** Using Q7's data, how many units give a profit of $12,000?
(a) 2,000  (b) 4,000  (c) 6,000  (d) 8,000

**10.** Using Q7's data, what is the result at 3,000 units?
(a) $6,000 profit  (b) $6,000 loss  (c) Break-even  (d) $18,000 profit

**11.** Fixed cost is $20,000, variable cost is $4 per unit, and you expect to sell 5,000 units. What price gives a $10,000 profit?
(a) $6  (b) $8  (c) $10  (d) $14

**12.** Making a part costs $60,000 a year fixed plus $12 per unit. Buying it costs $18 per unit. What is the indifference volume?
(a) 3,333  (b) 5,000  (c) 10,000  (d) 15,000

**13.** Using Q12's data, annual volume is 8,000 units. What should the firm do?
(a) Make  (b) Buy  (c) Either, the costs are equal  (d) Make, because fixed cost is ignored

**14.** Using Q12's data, at 15,000 units, which option is cheaper and by how much?
(a) Buy, by $30,000  (b) Make, by $30,000  (c) Make, by $90,000  (d) Buy, by $60,000

**15.** A supplier offers a part for $9. Making it in-house would cost $11 per unit plus a fixed cost. What should the firm do?
(a) Make if volume is high  (b) Buy at any volume  (c) Calculate the BEP first  (d) Make, because it's strategic

**16.** Option A has a fixed cost of $50,000 and a variable cost of $10. Option B has $80,000 and $7. At what volume do they cost the same, and which is better above it?
(a) 10,000, A  (b) 10,000, B  (c) 30,000, B  (d) 3,000, A

**17.** A firm builds a new plant because it expects demand to grow, and building takes three years. This is a:
(a) Following strategy  (b) Tracking strategy  (c) Leading strategy  (d) Defer strategy

**18.** A firm adds a small amount of capacity every few months to keep pace with demand. This is a:
(a) Leading strategy  (b) Tracking strategy  (c) Following strategy  (d) Make strategy

**19.** Management refuses to allow overtime or a second shift. Which determinant of effective capacity is this?
(a) Human  (b) Operational  (c) Policy  (d) External

**20.** One missing component stops a car assembly line. Which determinant is this?
(a) Facilities  (b) Operational  (c) Product  (d) Policy

**21.** On the make-or-buy matrix, a part with **low strategic value** and **low cost** should be:
(a) Made  (b) Bought  (c) Deferred  (d) Weighed on cost/benefit

**22.** Why do many projects start with a small pilot unit even though its operating cost is higher?
(a) Larger units have lower capital cost
(b) Larger units mean a big capital commitment before demand is proven
(c) Pilot units have higher utilisation
(d) Pilots avoid fixed costs completely

## C. Answers

| Q | Ans | Working / reason | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **a** | Efficiency = 85 ÷ 100 = 85%. Utilisation = 85 ÷ 120 = 70.8% | (b) swaps the two. Efficiency uses **effective**, utilisation uses **design** |
| 2 | **b** | Effective = 180. Actual = 0.9 × 180 = 162. Utilisation = 162 ÷ 200 = 81% | (a) forgot efficiency also cuts output |
| 3 | **c** | Planned allowances like breaks reduce **effective** capacity | (a), (b) and (d) are unplanned, so they reduce **actual** output |
| 4 | **d** | Quality problems are unplanned | The others are planned allowances |
| 5 | **c** | Hours = 1,800 + 1,800 + 1,200 = 4,800. Per machine = 8 × 2 × 250 = 4,000. 4,800 ÷ 4,000 = 1.2, round up to 2 | (b) didn't round up |
| 6 | **c** | 5,800 ÷ (8 × 250) = 2.9, round up to 3 | (b) didn't round up |
| 7 | **c** | 24,000 ÷ (15 − 9) = 4,000 | (a) divided by price, (b) divided by v |
| 8 | **a** | 5,000 × 6 − 24,000 = $6,000 | (b) forgot fixed cost |
| 9 | **c** | (12,000 + 24,000) ÷ 6 = 6,000 | (a) left out FC |
| 10 | **b** | 3,000 × 6 − 24,000 = −$6,000, below the BEP | — |
| 11 | **c** | 10,000 = 5,000(R − 4) − 20,000, so R − 4 = 6 and R = $10 | (a) forgot to add v back |
| 12 | **c** | 60,000 ÷ (18 − 12) = 10,000 | (b) 60,000 ÷ 12 |
| 13 | **b** | 8,000 is below the indifference volume, so buy | — |
| 14 | **b** | Make = 60,000 + 180,000 = 240,000. Buy = 270,000. Make saves $30,000 | — |
| 15 | **b** | The buy price is below your variable cost, so buy at any volume | — |
| 16 | **b** | 50,000 + 10Q = 80,000 + 7Q, so Q = 10,000. Above that, the lower variable cost wins: **B** | (a) picked the lower fixed cost |
| 17 | **c** | Capacity added **before** demand, with a long lead time | — |
| 18 | **b** | Small increments that keep pace with demand | (c) following waits until demand exceeds capacity |
| 19 | **c** | Overtime and shift rules are **policy** | — |
| 20 | **b** | Inventory shortage of one component is **operational** | (a) facilities is about size, layout and location |
| 21 | **b** | Low value and low cost means buy | — |
| 22 | **b** | Bigger capacity means higher capital cost, so firms test the market first | (a) is backwards |

**Mistakes to avoid:**
- Efficiency ÷ **effective**, utilisation ÷ **design**.
- Always **round up** machines.
- BEP uses **R − v**, not R.
- Target profit: **add** P to FC.
- In make-or-buy, above the indifference point choose the option with the **lower variable cost**.

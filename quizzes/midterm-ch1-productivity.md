# Chapter 1: Productivity & Competitiveness (Midterm Study Guide)

This guide is based on lecture 1 (Intro to EM & Operations) and lecture 2 (Productivity & Competitiveness), with the faculty's methods. Try Parts C and D on paper first, then check yourself against Parts E and F.

## A. Key ideas

- **Competitiveness:** an organisation's ability to deliver goods or services more effectively and efficiently than its rivals. It usually comes down to **cost, quality, speed and innovation**.
- **Operations** is named on the slides as the **number 1 reason organisations fail**.
- **Ten ways firms compete through operations:**
  1. Product and service design
  2. Cost
  3. Location
  4. Quality
  5. Quick response
  6. Flexibility
  7. Inventory management
  8. Supply chain management
  9. Service
  10. Managers and workers
- **Productivity = Output ÷ Input.** It measures how *efficiently* resources are used.
- **The three factors critical to improving productivity:** labour, capital and management.
- **Efficient vs effective:**
  - *Efficient* means doing things right, with minimum waste.
  - *Effective* means doing the right thing, i.e. having the right strategy.
- **Measurement problems:** quality changes, outside factors, and the lack of precise units.
- **Productivity factors:**
  - Methods, capital, quality, technology and management.
  - Standardisation, scrap rates, new workers, safety, layoffs, labour turnover, workspace design and incentive plans.
- **Layoffs:** productivity rises in the short term, then falls because of burnout, fear of further cuts, and the best people leaving.
- **Steps to improve productivity:**
  1. Develop measures.
  2. Find the bottleneck.
  3. Develop methods.
  4. Set reasonable goals.
  5. Get management support.
  6. Measure and publicise the results.
- **Process yield:** good output ÷ raw material input. For services it is, for example, cars rented ÷ cars available.
- **Cases:**
  - Toyota Kaizen: 500+ small improvements a month, about 4.2% productivity growth a year.
  - Samsung vs TSMC: Samsung had higher productivity, but TSMC's innovation won it a 54% market share.
  - Apple's MFP is higher than Samsung's because of design reuse and integration.

## B. Formula sheet

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Output ÷ Input |
| Labour productivity | Good units ÷ labour-hours |
| Multifactor productivity (MFP) | Output ÷ (Labour + Materials + Energy + Overhead + …), all in **$** |
| MFP in revenue terms | (Good units × price) ÷ total $ input |
| Productivity growth | (Current − Previous) ÷ Previous × 100% |
| Process yield | Good output ÷ raw input |
| Multi-stage yield | Yield₁ × Yield₂ × … |
| Labour-hours | Workers × hours per shift × shifts × days |

**Exam traps:**
1. Count **good units only**. Leave out defective or rejected ones.
2. Put every figure on the **same time basis** (day, week or year) before dividing.
3. **Include overhead.** It is often given as a % of labour cost.
4. Growth is a % of the **old** value.
5. Percentage points are not a percentage change.
6. A rise in single-factor productivity does **not** mean MFP has risen.

## C. Exam-style problems

**C1. Labour productivity with defects.** A factory runs 2 shifts of 8 hours, with 6 workers on each shift. It produces 1,920 units a day, and 4% are defective. Find the labour productivity.

**C2. MFP in units per dollar.** A workshop makes 4,500 units a week. Its weekly inputs are 5 workers × 40 h at $12/h, materials of $3,200, energy of $800, and overhead equal to 25% of labour cost. Find the MFP.

**C3. Revenue MFP and growth.** Each unit sells for $25.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Units sold | 10,000 | 11,000 |
| Labour | $80,000 | $84,000 |
| Materials | $60,000 | $66,000 |
| Overhead | $30,000 | $30,000 |

Find the MFP for each year in revenue per $ of input, and the productivity growth.

**C4. Should we invest?** A company makes 2,000 units a month, but 8% are defective and can't be sold. Good units sell for $50, and inputs cost $70,000 a month. A new quality system would cost $1,500 a month and cut defects to 2%. Does MFP improve? Show your working.

**C5. Single-factor vs MFP.** A line makes 1,200 units a month using 400 labour-hours at $15/h and $3,000 of materials. A robot leased for $2,000 a month would cut labour to 300 hours, with output unchanged. Find the change in labour productivity and in MFP, and comment.

**C6. Process yield.** A plant puts in 5,000 kg of raw material and gets 4,250 kg of good product.
- (a) What is the process yield?
- (b) Another product passes through two stages with yields of 92% and 95%. How many good units come out of 1,000 starts?

**C7. Mixed time units.** A bakery makes 300 loaves a day and is open 6 days a week. Its weekly costs are labour $5,400, materials $1,800 and overhead $1,000. Find the MFP.

**C8. Service productivity.** A bank has 3 tellers who each work 7 hours and serve 336 customers. One in eight customers has to come back because the problem wasn't solved. Find the productivity in resolved customers per labour-hour.

**C9. Working backwards.** A team produces 2,300 units using 460 labour-hours. Management wants labour productivity to rise by 15% with the same output. How many labour-hours must be cut?

## E. Solutions: Part C

**C1.**
- Labour-hours = 6 × 8 × 2 = 96 LH
- Good units = 1,920 × 0.96 = 1,843.2
- Productivity = 1,843.2 ÷ 96 = **19.2 units per labour-hour**
- *(Trap: if you count all 1,920 units you get 20.)*

**C2.**
- Labour = 5 × 40 × 12 = $2,400
- Overhead = 0.25 × 2,400 = $600
- Total input = 2,400 + 3,200 + 800 + 600 = $7,000
- MFP = 4,500 ÷ 7,000 = **0.643 units per $**
- *(Trap: leaving out overhead gives 0.703.)*

**C3.**
- Year 1: (10,000 × 25) ÷ (80,000 + 60,000 + 30,000) = 250,000 ÷ 170,000 = **1.471**
- Year 2: (11,000 × 25) ÷ (84,000 + 66,000 + 30,000) = 275,000 ÷ 180,000 = **1.528**
- Growth = (1.528 − 1.471) ÷ 1.471 × 100 = **+3.89%**

**C4.**
- Before: good units = 2,000 × 0.92 = 1,840. MFP = (1,840 × 50) ÷ 70,000 = 92,000 ÷ 70,000 = **1.314**
- After: good units = 2,000 × 0.98 = 1,960. MFP = (1,960 × 50) ÷ 71,500 = 98,000 ÷ 71,500 = **1.371**
- Change = **+4.29%**. **Yes, invest.** Inputs rise by only about 2% while sellable output rises by 6.5%.

**C5.**
- Labour productivity: 1,200 ÷ 400 = 3.0 before, 1,200 ÷ 300 = 4.0 after, so **+33.3%**.
- MFP before = 1,200 ÷ (6,000 + 3,000) = **0.1333**
- MFP after = 1,200 ÷ (4,500 + 3,000 + 2,000) = **0.1263**, so **−5.3%**.
- **Comment:** labour productivity looks better, but total productivity gets worse, because the $1,500 saved on labour is less than the $2,000 robot lease. Single-factor measures can mislead, so check MFP before deciding.

**C6.**
- (a) Yield = 4,250 ÷ 5,000 = **85%**
- (b) 0.92 × 0.95 = 0.874, so **874 good units**. *(Trap: 100% − 8% − 5% = 87%, giving 870, which is wrong.)*

**C7.**
- Weekly output = 300 × 6 = 1,800 loaves
- Weekly input = 5,400 + 1,800 + 1,000 = $8,200
- MFP = 1,800 ÷ 8,200 = **0.220 loaves per $**
- *(Trap: dividing daily output by weekly cost gives 0.037.)*

**C8.**
- Labour-hours = 3 × 7 = 21
- Resolved customers = 336 × 7/8 = 294
- Productivity = 294 ÷ 21 = **14 resolved customers per labour-hour**

**C9.**
- Current productivity = 2,300 ÷ 460 = 5.0. Target = 5.0 × 1.15 = 5.75
- Hours needed = 2,300 ÷ 5.75 = 400
- Cut = 460 − 400 = **60 labour-hours**

## F. Answer points: Part D

1. **Competitiveness:** delivering goods or services more effectively and efficiently than rivals. Any four of the ten operations factors work, e.g. cost, quality, quick response, flexibility.
2. **Efficiency vs effectiveness:**
   - Efficiency is doing a job with minimum resources and waste. Example: the same 1,000 units made with 10% less material.
   - Effectiveness is choosing and achieving the right goals. Example: spotting EV demand and launching an EV line.
3. **Misleading measurement:** quality may change while quantity stays the same, outside factors may interfere, and precise units may be missing. Comparing a factory today with one 30 years ago shows the problem.
4. **Layoffs:**
   - Short term, productivity rises: the same work is done by fewer people.
   - Long term, it may fall: burnout, fear of more cuts, and the most capable workers leave.
5. **Improvement steps:**
   1. Develop productivity measures.
   2. Find the critical (bottleneck) operations.
   3. Develop methods for improvement.
   4. Set reasonable goals.
   5. Get management support.
   6. Measure and publicise the improvements.
6. **Not always.** Samsung was more productive (12% more chips per wafer), but TSMC competed on innovation (3 nm chips) and took a 54% market share. Productivity is one route to competitiveness. Innovation, quality and speed are others.
7. **Process yield:** good output ÷ raw input.
   - Manufacturing: good kg of product per kg of raw material.
   - Service: cars rented ÷ cars available. Admissions offers accepted ÷ offers made also works.
8. **Same unit for MFP:** inputs like hours, kg and kWh can't be added together. Converting them all to dollars gives one comparable total for the denominator.

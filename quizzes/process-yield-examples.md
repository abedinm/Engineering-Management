# Process Yield: Worked Examples

**Process yield = Good output ÷ Input**

Process yield shows how much of what goes into a process comes out as good, usable output. For services it is measured as, for example, cars rented ÷ cars available.

| You want | Do this |
|---|---|
| Yield | Good output ÷ Input |
| Good output | Input × Yield |
| Input needed | Good output required ÷ Yield |
| Overall yield of several stages | Yield₁ × Yield₂ × Yield₃ … |
| Change in good output | (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100% |
| Cost per good unit | Total cost ÷ Good units |

## Example 1: Basic yield

A bakery uses 1,800 kg of flour and gets 1,710 kg of sellable bread.

Yield = 1,710 ÷ 1,800 = **0.95, or 95%**

## Example 2: Good output from a known yield

A machine has a 90% yield, and 2,400 parts are started.

Good parts = 2,400 × 0.90 = **2,160**

## Example 3: Several stages

A phone passes through three stages with yields of 95%, 90% and 98%. 5,000 phones are started.

- Overall yield = 0.95 × 0.90 × 0.98 = **0.8379, or 83.79%**
- Good phones = 5,000 × 0.8379 ≈ **4,190**

> **Trap:** subtracting the losses (100 − 5 − 10 − 2 = 83%) is wrong. Each stage only works on what the previous stage passed, so the yields multiply.

## Example 4: Working backwards to the input needed

You need 4,600 good units, and the yield is 92%.

Input needed = 4,600 ÷ 0.92 = **5,000 units**

## Example 5: Service yield

A car rental agency has 120 cars available, and 84 are rented today.

Yield = 84 ÷ 120 = **70%**

Other service examples: hotel rooms booked ÷ rooms available, or admission offers accepted ÷ students approved for admission.

## Example 6: Yield improvement as a % change

A line starts 1,000 units a day. Better maintenance raises its yield from 88% to 95%.

- Good output goes from 1,000 × 0.88 = 880 to 1,000 × 0.95 = 950, an extra **70 units a day**
- Change in good output = (950 − 880) ÷ 880 × 100 = **+7.95%**

> **Trap:** 95% − 88% = 7 *percentage points*, which is not a 7% increase.

## Example 7: Cost per good unit

Making 1,000 units costs $10,000.

| Yield | Good units | Cost per good unit |
|---|---|---|
| 90% | 900 | $10,000 ÷ 900 = **$11.11** |
| 96% | 960 | $10,000 ÷ 960 = **$10.42** |

A higher yield lowers the cost of every good unit. That's why yield is tied to productivity and to cost competitiveness.

## Practice questions

1. A plant puts in 2,500 kg of raw material and gets 2,150 kg of good product. What is the process yield?
2. A product passes through two stages with yields of 97% and 92%. How many good units come out of 3,000 starts?
3. A company needs 1,800 good units, and its process yield is 90%. How many units must it start?
4. A hotel has 150 rooms, and 123 are booked tonight. What is the yield?
5. A line starts 1,500 units a day. Its yield rises from 80% to 90%. By what percentage does good output increase?

**Answers:**
1. 2,150 ÷ 2,500 = **86%**
2. 0.97 × 0.92 = 0.8924, and 3,000 × 0.8924 ≈ **2,677 units**
3. 1,800 ÷ 0.90 = **2,000 units**
4. 123 ÷ 150 = **82%**
5. 1,200 to 1,350 good units: (1,350 − 1,200) ÷ 1,200 = **+12.5%**. That's 10 percentage points, but a 12.5% increase.

# Quiz 1: Analytical MCQs, Set 2 (Productivity & Forecasting)

20 more calculation and reasoning questions, all different from Set 1. Several ask you to **work backwards**: find the missing input, α or demand value. Every wrong option is a common mistake.
Aim for about **1.5 minutes per question**. Solutions start on the next page.

## Part A: Productivity

**1.** A shop sells 500 units at $40 each. Its inputs are labour (5 workers × 8 h at $25/h), materials $3,000, energy $500 and capital $1,500. What is the MFP in **revenue per dollar of input**?
(a) 0.083  (b) 3.33  (c) 4.00  (d) 20.0

**2.** Labour productivity was 4.0 in 2023, 4.4 in 2024 and 4.62 in 2025. What is the **total** growth from 2023 to 2025?
(a) 15.0%  (b) 15.5%  (c) 5.0%  (d) 0.62%

**3.** A team produces 1,200 units in 300 labour-hours. The goal is a **20% rise in labour productivity** with the same output. How many labour-hours must be cut?
(a) 60  (b) 50  (c) 240  (d) 20

**4.** Plant A produces 2,000 units from $5,000 of inputs. Plant B produces 3,300 units from $9,000. Which statement is correct?
(a) B is more productive, because it makes more units
(b) A is more productive by about 9.1%
(c) B is more productive by about 9.1%
(d) They are equally productive

**5.** A line makes 1,000 units with 200 labour-hours at $20/h and $2,000 of materials. A new machine costs **$1,500 per period** and cuts labour to 160 hours. Output stays at 1,000. What happens?
(a) Labour productivity +25% and MFP +25%
(b) Labour productivity +25% and MFP about −10.4%
(c) Labour productivity −20% and MFP +10.4%
(d) Both stay unchanged, because output didn't change

**6.** A part passes through two stages. Stage 1 yield is 95% and stage 2 yield is 90%. How many good parts come out of 1,000 starts?
(a) 850  (b) 855  (c) 900  (d) 925

**7.** A call centre has 4 agents working 8 hours, who handle 384 calls. 25% of calls are **not resolved** and need a callback. Productivity in **resolved calls per labour-hour** is:
(a) 12  (b) 9  (c) 72  (d) 3

**8.** A firm makes 2,000 units from $4,000 of inputs. An upgrade would raise output by 8%. What is the **most** it can spend per period on the upgrade without lowering MFP?
(a) $80  (b) $160  (c) $320  (d) $400

## Part B: Forecasting

**9.** The period-1 forecast is 50 and α = 0.2. Actual demand was 54, 49 and 58 in periods 1, 2 and 3. What is the forecast for **period 4**?
(a) 50.44  (b) 51.95  (c) 52.27  (d) 53.40

**10.** Last period's forecast was 80 and actual demand was 90. The new forecast is 84. What smoothing constant α was used?
(a) 0.2  (b) 0.4  (c) 0.6  (d) 0.84

**11.** A 3-month moving average gave a forecast of **40** for month 6. Demand in months 3 and 4 was 36 and 40. What was demand in **month 5**?
(a) 40  (b) 42  (c) 44  (d) 38

**12.** Demand was 100 for five periods, then jumped to **150** in period 6. What do a 2-period and a 5-period MA forecast for period 7?
(a) 125 and 110  (b) 150 and 150  (c) 110 and 125  (d) 125 and 120

**13.** The quarterly trend line is F(t) = 200 + 15t. Q4 of next year is t = 8 and has a seasonal relative of 1.15. What is the seasonalised forecast?
(a) 320  (b) 368  (c) 338  (d) 278

**14.** Average quarterly demand is 400 units, and Q2 averages 460 units. What is Q2's seasonal relative, and what does it mean?
(a) 0.87, so Q2 is 13% below average
(b) 1.15, so Q2 is 15% above average
(c) 60, so Q2 is 60 units above average
(d) 1.15, so Q2 is 15% below average

**15.** The correlation between ad spending and sales is r = 0.8. What share of the variation in sales does ad spending explain?
(a) 80%  (b) 64%  (c) 40%  (d) 8%

**16.** Forecast errors (A − F) over six periods were −3, +5, −2, +4, −6 and +2. Which statement is correct?
(a) MAD = 0, so the forecast is perfect
(b) TS = 0 and MAD ≈ 3.67, so there is no bias, though errors still exist
(c) TS = 6, so the forecast is biased
(d) MAD ≈ 3.67, so the forecast is biased

**17.** Product X sells about 100 units with MAD = 10. Product Y sells about 1,000 units with MAD = 50. Which forecast is relatively **more accurate**?
(a) X, because its MAD is smaller
(b) Y, because its MAPE is about 5% compared with 10% for X
(c) X, because its MAPE is about 5%
(d) They can't be compared

**18.** A 2-period weighted MA uses weights 0.6 for the most recent period and 0.4 for the one before. Last period's demand was 50, and the forecast came out at 46. What was demand in the period before?
(a) 40  (b) 42  (c) 44  (d) 46

**19.** A forecast has MSE = 16. A control chart for errors uses **±2σ** limits, where σ ≈ √MSE. Next period's error is +10. What should you conclude?
(a) Within limits (±32), no action needed
(b) Outside limits (±8), so investigate the forecast
(c) Within limits (±16), no action needed
(d) Outside limits (±4), so investigate the forecast

**20.** A *naive forecast with trend* adds the last change to the last actual. Demand was 50 last month and 56 this month. What is next month's forecast?
(a) 56  (b) 53  (c) 62  (d) 106

## Solutions

| Q | Ans | Working | Trap options |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **b** | Labour = 5 × 8 × 25 = $1,000. Inputs = 1,000 + 3,000 + 500 + 1,500 = $6,000. Revenue = 500 × 40 = $20,000. 20,000 ÷ 6,000 = **3.33** | (a) units per $, not revenue. (d) revenue ÷ labour only |
| 2 | **b** | 4.62 ÷ 4.0 − 1 = **15.5%**. The rises compound: 1.10 × 1.05 = 1.155 | (a) added 10% + 5%. (c) last year only |
| 3 | **b** | 1,200 ÷ 300 = 4.0. Target = 4.8. Hours = 1,200 ÷ 4.8 = 250. Cut = **50** | (a) cut hours by 20%: 300 × 0.8 = 240, a cut of 60, which raises productivity by 25%, not 20% |
| 4 | **b** | A = 0.400, B = 0.367. 0.400 ÷ 0.367 = 1.091, so A is **9.1% higher** | (a) mistook volume for productivity |
| 5 | **b** | Labour: 1,000 ÷ 200 = 5 → 1,000 ÷ 160 = 6.25, which is **+25%**. MFP: 1,000 ÷ 6,000 = 0.1667 → 1,000 ÷ (3,200 + 2,000 + 1,500) = 0.1493, which is **−10.4%** | Single-factor productivity can rise while MFP falls. That's why you check both |
| 6 | **b** | Yields multiply: 0.95 × 0.90 = 0.855. 1,000 × 0.855 = **855** | (a) subtracted the losses: 100% − 5% − 10% = 85% |
| 7 | **b** | Resolved calls = 384 × 0.75 = 288. Labour-hours = 32. 288 ÷ 32 = **9** | (a) counted unresolved calls too. (c) per agent |
| 8 | **c** | MFP now = 2,000 ÷ 4,000 = 0.5. New output = 2,160. Inputs allowed = 2,160 ÷ 0.5 = 4,320. Spend ≤ **$320** | (b) halved it. (d) assumed output +10% |
| 9 | **b** | F₂ = 50 + 0.2(54 − 50) = 50.8. F₃ = 50.8 + 0.2(49 − 50.8) = 50.44. F₄ = 50.44 + 0.2(58 − 50.44) = **51.95** | (a) stopped one step early. (c) sign slip in step 2 |
| 10 | **b** | 84 = 80 + α(90 − 80). α = 4 ÷ 10 = **0.4** | (c) 1 − α. (d) mixed up α with F |
| 11 | **c** | (36 + 40 + x) ÷ 3 = 40, so x = 120 − 76 = **44** | (a) assumed month 5 equals the forecast |
| 12 | **a** | 2-period: (100 + 150) ÷ 2 = **125**. 5-period: (4 × 100 + 150) ÷ 5 = **110** | Fewer periods react faster to the jump |
| 13 | **b** | Trend = 200 + 15 × 8 = 320. 320 × 1.15 = **368** | (a) forgot the seasonal relative. (d) divided by it |
| 14 | **b** | 460 ÷ 400 = **1.15**. Above 1 means above average | (a) inverted: 400 ÷ 460. (c) a difference, not a ratio |
| 15 | **b** | r² = 0.8² = **0.64**, so 64% | (a) used r instead of r² |
| 16 | **b** | Σe = 0, so TS = 0 and there's no bias. Σ\|e\| = 22, MAD = 22 ÷ 6 = 3.67 | Bias and accuracy are different things: you can be unbiased and still inaccurate |
| 17 | **b** | MAPE for X ≈ 10 ÷ 100 = 10%. For Y ≈ 50 ÷ 1,000 = 5%. Y is relatively better | (a) compared MADs on different scales. Use MAPE for that |
| 18 | **a** | 0.6(50) + 0.4x = 46, so 0.4x = 16 and x = **40** | (d) assumed both periods equal the forecast |
| 19 | **b** | σ = √16 = 4. Limits = ±2 × 4 = ±8. An error of +10 is outside them | (c) used MSE as σ. (a) multiplied MSE by 2 |

**Score guide:** 17–20 means you're ready. 13–16 means re-check the trap column for the ones you missed. Below 13, go back over the formulas in the full prep sheet.

**New traps in this set**
- Growth rates **compound**: multiply them, don't add them.
- Yields across stages **multiply**.
- A 20% productivity rise is **not** the same as a 20% cut in hours.
- To find α, use α = (F_new − F_old) ÷ (A − F_old).
- Use **r²** for explained variation, not r.
- Compare accuracy across scales with **MAPE**, not MAD.
- TS = 0 means no bias, not no error.
- Control limits use **σ = √MSE**.

## Goods vs services

- **Goods:** physical items. Raw materials, parts, subassemblies (e.g. motherboards) and final products (phones, cars).
- **Services:** activities that provide some combination of **time, location, form or psychological value**.
- Most outputs sit on a **continuum**, from pure goods (car assembly, steelmaking) through mixes (home remodelling and retail, car repair and fast food, computer repair and restaurant meals, songwriting and software) to near-pure services (surgery, teaching).

| Characteristic | Goods | Services |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Tangible | Intangible |
| Customer contact | Low | High |
| Labour content | Low | High |
| Uniformity of input | High | Low |
| Measuring productivity | Easy | Difficult |
| Chance to fix problems before delivery | High | Low |
| Inventory | Much | Little |
| Wages | Narrow range | Wide range |
| Patentable | Usually | Not usually |

## The organisation

- **Organisation:** people working together and coordinating their actions to reach goals, using resources (people, machinery, raw materials, information, skills, financial capital).
- **Goal:** a *broad* desired outcome over **3–5 years**, e.g. maximise profit, become an industry leader, become carbon-neutral. This is **where you want to go**.
- **Objective:** a *specific, measurable* target that helps reach a goal, e.g. raise sales by 15% or cut defects by 10%. These are **the measurable steps to get there**.
- **Management:** getting people together to accomplish goals and objectives.
- **Manager:** someone who coordinates and oversees other people's work so the organisation reaches its goals.

## Levels of management and skills

| Level | Who they manage | Skill mix |
|---|---|---|
| **Top managers** | Make organisation-wide decisions, plans and goals | Mostly **conceptual** |
| **Middle managers** | First-line managers | Balanced |
| **First-line managers** | Non-managerial employees | Mostly **technical** |

**Human** skills matter about equally at every level.

- **Technical skills:** knowledge and proficiency in a specific field.
- **Human skills:** the ability to work well with other people.
- **Conceptual skills:** the ability to think through abstract, complex situations affecting the organisation.

## Competitive advantage: the best companies deliver all four

**Competitive advantage** is a market position that lets a company earn a return on investment *higher than the cost of that investment*. It comes from four sources:

| Source | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Cost competitiveness** | Costs low enough to make a profit at attractive prices. The key is **efficiency** |
| **Quality** | Attractiveness, no defects, reliability, durability |
| **Speed** | Often separates winners from losers. Became vital in the 1990s |
| **Innovation** | New goods and services. Lets you adapt to changing demand and new competitors |

## Efficiency vs effectiveness (organisational performance)

| | **Efficiency (means)** | **Effectiveness (ends)** |
|---|---|---|
| What it measures | How well **resources** are used | Whether the **right goals** were chosen and **achieved** |
| Aim | Low waste: minimum input for the same goal | High goal attainment |
| Example | The same 1,000 units made with 10% less material and energy | Spotting EV demand and launching a successful EV line |

Management aims for **both**: low resource waste *and* high goal attainment.

## The four managerial functions (P-O-L-C)

| Function | What it is | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | Set goals, choose strategies, develop plans. Three steps: *which goals? how to reach them? how to allocate resources?* | Determines the organisation's effectiveness, efficiency and strategy |
| **Organising** | Decide what needs doing, how, and by whom. Group people into departments and set lines of authority | An **organisational structure** |
| **Leading** | Set the direction and vision, and motivate people using power, influence, persuasion and communication | High **motivation and commitment** |
| **Controlling** | Monitor individuals, departments and the organisation, and take corrective action | **Accurate measurement** of performance and regulation of efficiency and effectiveness |

## Managerial roles (Mintzberg)

A **role** is a set of tasks a person performs because of their position. Roles point both inside and outside the organisation.

| Category | Roles |
|---|---|
| **Interpersonal** (people and ceremonial duties) | Figurehead, leader, liaison |
| **Informational** (collect and share information) | Monitor, disseminator, spokesperson |
| **Decisional** (make choices) | Entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator |

That makes 3 + 3 + 4 = **10 roles**.

## Operations management

- Every business has **three basic functions: finance, operations and marketing**.
- **Operations** is the part of the organisation that **produces goods and/or services**. It needs support and input from the other areas.
- **Transformation model:**
  - **Inputs** (land, labour, capital, information) → **transformation/conversion process** → **outputs** (goods and services).
  - **Control** uses measurement and feedback at every stage.
- **Value added** = the value or price of outputs − the cost of inputs.
  - **For-profit:** value is what customers are willing to pay.
  - **Non-profit:** value is the value to society, e.g. highways or police. Greater value added means more effective operations.
  - Firms use value added to fund R&D, new facilities, salaries and profit. Value can also be **psychological** (branding).
- **Transformation examples:**
  - **Food processor:** raw vegetables, metal sheets, water, energy and labour → cleaning, making cans, cutting, cooking, packing, labelling → **canned vegetables**.
  - **Hospital:** doctors, nurses, supplies, equipment and labs → examination, surgery, monitoring, medication, therapy → **treated patients**.

## Supply chain management

- **Supply chain:** the sequence of organisations, and their facilities, functions and activities, involved in producing and delivering a product or service.
- **Simple chain:** suppliers' suppliers → direct suppliers → producer → distributor → final customers.
- **Facilities:** warehouses, factories, processing centres, offices, distribution centres, retail outlets.
- **Functions:** forecasting, purchasing, inventory, information management, quality assurance, scheduling, production, distribution, delivery, customer service.
- **Bread example:** farm → mill → bakery → supermarket, with trucking and suppliers at every stage. **Value increases as the product moves along the chain.**
- **Operations and supply chains are intrinsically linked.** No business can exist without both.

## Scope of operations management

- **OM covers:** forecasting, capacity planning, scheduling, inventory management, quality assurance, motivating employees, facility location, SCM and more.
- **Interfaces with:** industrial engineering, maintenance, public relations, personnel, MIS, accounting, legal, purchasing and distribution.
